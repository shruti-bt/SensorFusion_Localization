# Localization method and literature review

## Method (short paragraph)

We localize the ROV in the ship frame by fusing dead reckoning with acoustic positioning in a single factor graph, solved as one batch optimisation. Dead reckoning uses the relative poses of the ZED Mini visual-inertial odometry (VIO), the DVL velocity integrated between consecutive images (which gives metric scale), the ROV roll and pitch (which give the direction of gravity) and the pressure-sensor depth. These sensors give a smooth, metric trajectory, but position along the hull and heading drift, and there is no compass because the steel hull disturbs it. Each Water Linked Underwater GPS fix adds an absolute-position factor with a robust (Huber) loss, weighted by the unit's reported standard deviation, and corrected for the offset of the acoustic locator on the ROV. Because the VIO frame is arbitrary, the optimisation also estimates the alignment of that frame to the receiver frame (a yaw about gravity and a translation); the ship frame then follows from the known mounting of the receiver on the ship. The output is the ROV pose in the ship frame for every image, with a position uncertainty that includes the alignment. In simulation the position error in the ship frame was 0.04 m RMSE with the acoustic fixes, against 0.32 m for dead reckoning alone; the method has not yet been tested on real data.

## Literature review

**Underwater navigation and the need for absolute fixes.** GPS and radio signals do not propagate underwater, so underwater vehicles rely on dead reckoning from inertial sensors and a DVL, which drifts without bound, and on acoustic positioning or SLAM to bound that drift (Paull et al., 2014). Acoustic systems such as USBL give an absolute position relative to a topside transceiver and do not drift, but are noisier and suffer from outliers caused by multipath and loss of line of sight. A common solution is to fuse them with inertial and DVL data. Recent work formulates INS/USBL/DVL fusion as factor-graph optimisation and adds robust handling of acoustic outliers, for example through correntropy-based or Mahalanobis-distance anomaly handling, and reports gains over Kalman-filter fusion (Sensors 2023, 23(2), 916; Dong et al., RMIT). Our localization follows this line: dead reckoning plus an outlier-tolerant absolute position factor in one graph.

**Factor graphs and visual-inertial odometry.** Factor graphs are now the standard way to combine heterogeneous sensors, since each sensor becomes a factor on shared state variables and robust losses can be applied per factor (Dellaert and Kaess, 2017; Kaess et al., 2012, for incremental solving). Visual-inertial odometry within this framework, including on-manifold IMU preintegration, is well established (Forster et al., 2017). Underwater, vision is degraded by haze, low light and lighting changes, so systems add acoustic and pressure sensing. SVIn2 fuses a profiling sonar, visual, inertial and water-pressure data in a tightly coupled optimisation and uses depth to initialise scale (Rahman et al., 2022). We take the ZED VIO as an input, as it is, and add DVL, gravity, pressure and acoustic factors around it, rather than building a new VIO.

**Ship hull inspection.** Hull-relative navigation has a long history, starting with hull-relative navigation and control (Vaganay et al., 2005) and the HAUV system with its perception, navigation and planning (Hover et al., 2012). On featureless hull surfaces, visual SLAM has to cope with limited field of view and few features: Kim and Eustice (2013) use the navigation prior in image registration and select only informative images through a saliency measure, and demonstrate real-time visual SLAM on hull inspection surveys of up to 3.4 h and 2.7 km. That work and ours address the same difficulty, but we handle it by adding an absolute acoustic reference instead of relying on the images alone.

**Mapping and localizing against a map.** For the mapping side of this project we use COLMAP, an incremental structure-from-motion system (Schönberger and Frahm, 2016), with known poses from the fused trajectory. Once such a map exists, a new image can be localized in it by retrieving similar mapped images and matching local features, as in hierarchical localization (Sarlin et al., 2019). Such map-based fixes would add further absolute factors to the same graph and would help most where the acoustic fixes are weak. They are not implemented yet.

**Position of this work.** The localization here is a straightforward composition of established components (factor-graph fusion, robust absolute position factors, a joint estimate of the frame alignment) applied to the specific sensor set of a BlueROV with a ZED Mini and Water Linked UGPS. We do not claim a new estimator. The open questions are practical: how well the noise model holds near a steel hull, where multipath and shadowing are expected, and how much a map-based fix adds on featureless paint. Both need real recordings.
